🧭 Before you begin: two small icons to notice

Across the dashboard, you'll spot two small visual cues that make everything easier to understand at a glance:

The All-Time badge — dashed border, light grey background

A handful of cards (like Bank Balance, Contingency Cash, and Corpus) are marked "All-Time." This means they always show the complete picture from day one until today — changing the date filter at the top of the page will not change these numbers. Every other card responds to whatever date range you've picked.

The little blue arrow (→) in the corner of a card

If you see a small circular arrow tucked into the corner of a card, that card is clickable — tap or click it to jump straight into a detailed, line-by-line breakdown of that number.

🏠 Overview

Your one-page summary — if you only ever look at one screen, make it this one.

Summary

The default tab -- a one-line "at a glance" readout of income vs. expense vs. surplus for your selected period, plus three compact cards (Net Position, Collection Progress, Income vs Expense) and a Reserves · Bank Balance bar showing the Contingency / Corpus / Unrestricted split.

Collections & Spending

The full numbers behind the summary: Collection Health (Expected vs Collected Maintenance, all-time Outstanding Receivables, Other Income) and Society Financial Position (Opening Balance, Total Income/Expense, Net Operating Surplus), plus Top 5 charts for income sources, expense categories, and vendors -- click any bar to drill down.

Reserves & Long-term

Long-Term Financial Strength: Corpus with Interest (the sinking fund), Contingency Cash (the ring-fenced emergency reserve), Bank Balance (the real all-time cash position), and the Maintenance Rate Trend for your selected range.

Audited Report

The society's official, professionally audited financial report, viewable or downloadable right here for whichever financial year you pick -- the same document your management committee is required to share with all residents.

📄 Audited Report

The society's official, professionally audited financial report for the year, prepared by an independent chartered accountant. You can view it right here or download it as a PDF — this is the same document your management committee is legally required to share with all residents.

🔵 Collection Health

Expected Collection

The target amount the society should collect this period, based on the maintenance rate (per square foot) multiplied by the total built-up area of all flats. Think of it as "how much we're supposed to collect."

Collected Maintenance

How much maintenance money has actually been received from residents so far, for the period you're viewing.

Outstanding Dues All-Time

The total unpaid maintenance dues built up across the society's entire history — money residents still owe, carried forward month after month. This is deliberately shown as an all-time figure so it always reflects the true, complete picture of what's pending, no matter which date range you're browsing.

Other Income

Money the society earns from things other than maintenance — renting the community hall, parking charges, late-payment penalties, advertising space, and similar sources.

🟢 Society Financial Position

Total Income Click for details

Maintenance collected plus all other income, combined. Click this card to see exactly which categories make up this total.

Total Expense Click for details

Everything the society spent during the period — staff salaries, electricity, water, housekeeping, repairs, and more. Click to see the full breakdown.

Net Operating Surplus

Total Income minus Total Expense. Shown in green with "Positive" when the society saved money during the period; shown in red as "Net Operating Deficit" if it spent more than it earned.

Recovery Rate

What percentage of the Expected Collection target was actually collected. 90% or higher is considered Healthy; lower percentages are flagged as Watch or At Risk, prompting the management committee to follow up with residents who haven't paid.

🟠 Long-Term Financial Strength

Corpus with Interest (Accumulated) All-Time

The society's long-term savings fund (sometimes called a "sinking fund") — money set aside over the years for major future repairs, like repainting the building or replacing lifts.

Contingency Cash All-Time Click for trend

A small portion of every resident's maintenance payment is deliberately set aside as an emergency reserve, separate from day-to-day operating funds. Important: this money is not extra cash on top of the Bank Balance — it's already included inside it, just ring-fenced for emergencies. Click this card to see how the reserve has grown month by month.

Bank Balance All-Time

The actual amount of cash the society holds right now, added up from the very first day of digital record-keeping until today. Because it's "all-time," this figure includes the Contingency Cash reserve described above — it isn't a separate pot of money.

Expense / Income Ratio

What percentage of income has been spent, for your selected period. A colored bar and label (Healthy / Caution / Over Budget) give you an instant read on whether spending is under control.

📊 Top 5 charts (expense categories, income sources, vendors)

Three quick-glance charts showing the society's biggest expense categories, its top income sources (excluding maintenance), and the vendors paid the most. Click any bar to jump straight into a detailed breakdown for that specific category or vendor.

💧 Cashflow Health

A closer look at how money moves in and out of the society, month by month.

Aggregate, community-level view

Everything on this page reflects the whole society combined -- there's no individual flat-wise tracking here. Head to the Head Drill-down page if you need to trace a specific vendor or category down to individual transactions.

Expense / Income

The same measurement you saw on the Overview page — what percentage of the money coming in is being spent, for your selected date range.

Surplus months / Deficit months

Out of all the months in your selected range, how many months did the society end up saving money (Surplus), and how many months did it spend more than it earned (Deficit)?

Collection performance vs target

Compares actual maintenance collected against the Expected Collection target, shown as a percentage difference. A number like "−7.9%" means collection came in just under 8% short of the target for that period.

Best & worst month (net)

Within your selected range, the single best month (highest savings) and single worst month (lowest savings or biggest shortfall) are called out directly, so you don't have to hunt for them in the chart below.

📈 Monthly trend · total income, expense & outstanding dues

This chart deliberately shows total income from every source (not maintenance alone) against Total Expense and the cumulative Outstanding Dues balance — the full cash-flow picture for the society. For a maintenance-specific breakdown of collected vs. unpaid amounts, see the Income Visibility page below, which owns that detail so it's never shown twice.

💰 Income Visibility

Where every rupee of income actually comes from.

Income sources

Maintenance Collections is called out separately at the top since it's usually the largest source of income by far. Below it, every other income source — rent, events, penalties, advertising, and more — is ranked from largest to smallest in a simple bar chart.

Expense / Income

A compact summary card showing total income, total expense, and whether the period ended in Surplus or Deficit — the same calculation used everywhere else on the dashboard.

Collected Maintenance / Unpaid Maintenance / Recovery Rate / Months with Dues

Four headline figures for the selected period, giving you the summary before you look at the month-by-month detail in the two charts below.

📊 Collected vs unpaid maintenance

Shows, month by month: how much maintenance was collected (green bars), how much remained unpaid (red bars), and the Expected Collection target (dashed line) — a clean, currency-only view of whether the society is hitting its per-square-foot collection target.

📈 Recovery rate trend

Shown as its own dedicated chart, right next to the collection chart: what percentage of due maintenance was actually recovered, month by month, compared against a 90% "healthy" benchmark line. Keeping this separate from the currency chart above makes both easier to read at a glance.

🏦 Opening & Closing Balance

Think of this page as the society's running bank passbook.

Opening Balance

How this is calculated: the starting amount recorded when the society first began tracking finances digitally, plus every rupee saved (income minus expenses) from that date up to the start of whichever period you're viewing. A small badge next to this figure tells you whether it's a real, confirmed starting figure or our best estimate (see the note just below the balance strip for details).

Total Income / Total Expense / Net Operating Surplus

How much came in, how much went out, and the difference between the two, for your selected period.

Closing Balance

Opening Balance plus Net Operating Surplus — the society's cash position at the end of your selected period. This becomes next period's Opening Balance.

Months of expense covered

A simple "safety cushion" indicator: if the society stopped collecting any income today, how many months could it keep paying its regular expenses using only the money it currently has? A higher number means a stronger financial safety net.

Contingency reserve within closing balance

Just like on the Overview page, this shows what portion of the Closing Balance is the ring-fenced emergency reserve versus money that's freely available for regular society operations.

📋 Month-by-month continuity table

A transparent, line-by-line ledger: for every month, Opening + Income − Expense = Closing, and that Closing amount automatically becomes the next month's Opening. This is the same simple math your household budget or a bank passbook uses — just applied to the whole society.

📈 Monthly contingency fund collection

Tracks how much was added to the emergency reserve each month (bars) and the running total built up over time (line) — the same reserve referenced in the Contingency Cash card on Overview.

🔍 Head Drill-down

The "zoom in" page — follow any number all the way down to the smallest transaction.

Step 1 — Choose a Head

Start on the Heads screen and pick Expense or Income -- each card shows how many categories sit underneath it and the selected period's total.

Step 2 — Income Categories

Every income category (Maintenance Collection, Penalties, Commercial Income, and more) listed with its vendor count and period total, largest first. Click any row to go deeper.

Step 2 — Expense Categories

The same breakdown for expenses (Facility Management, Utilities, Petty Cash, and more). A category with more than one vendor (like "Utilities · 4 vendors") shows a vendor list next, before the final trend chart.

Step 3 — Combined Monthly Trend

The final level of detail: a month-by-month bar chart for that category (here, Facility Rental income) plus the total for the selected period. If a category has only one vendor, this is the very next screen after Step 2.

Same view, for expenses

The equivalent trend chart on the expense side (here, Facility Management) -- hover any bar to see that exact month's value.

How to use this page

Start by choosing Expense or Income. From there, simply keep clicking to go deeper:

Category (e.g. "Utilities") → Vendor (only shown if the category has more than one -- e.g. "BESCOM") → the final combined monthly trend chart, with every underlying transaction accounted for. Every list is sorted from largest amount to smallest, so the biggest numbers are always at the top, easiest to spot. Use the breadcrumb trail at the top of the page to jump back up a level at any time.

🔮 Forecasting

A what-if explorer built from your society's own real numbers -- and every single number on this page can be verified, not just trusted.

What this page is (and isn't)

Forecasting shows a full financial year as two kinds of months: the ones that have already happened (Actual -- real recorded numbers, never estimated) and the ones still ahead (Forecast -- projected using the assumptions you set below). Every slider on this page is yours to move -- residents and admins alike -- and nothing you change here is saved or shown to anyone else. Reload the page and it resets to the defaults. Think of it as a calculator, not an official budget.

🧮 See the exact live calculation behind any number

Wherever you see a small circled "i" button next to a lever or slider, click it. A popup opens showing the exact formula behind that number -- using the real baseline, your current lever settings, and the actual result -- all live, all pulled from the same numbers driving the page. For the headline result tiles (Opening, Closing, Net Surplus), look instead for the "📊 See Full Calculation" button, which opens the same kind of live, step-by-step breakdown. This exists specifically so nobody has to just take the forecast on faith: if a number looks surprising, open its calculation and see precisely how it was built.

This is available on: the Opening, Closing, and Net Surplus tiles, the Maintenance Rate and Contingency Rate sliders, and every individual income and expense category lever. If you ever want to double-check a specific category's forecasted contribution, its own "i" button shows exactly which baseline, which growth %, and which inflation multiplier produced that month's number.

Fiscal Year selector

At the top right, choose which financial year to look at: a closed year (fully actual, nothing forecast), the current year (a mix of actual and forecast months), or a future year (fully forecast). The table and charts below update to match whichever year you pick.

Global Assumptions

Six numbers that apply across the whole forecast -- click the "i" next to Maintenance Rate or Contingency Rate to see their exact formula live:

  • Maintenance Rate (₹/sqft/mo) -- the per-square-foot monthly rate used to calculate maintenance billing for forecast months only. Formula: Rate × total built-up area (sqft) × Collection %. Actual months always use the real billed amount, never this slider. Range is deliberately narrowed to a realistic ₹3–₹5/sqft band, in 0.05 steps, so it can't be dragged to an implausible value by accident.
  • Maintenance Collection % -- what share of billed maintenance you expect to actually collect (100% would mean every rupee billed gets paid on time).
  • Interest on Surplus % -- a small extra income assumption applied to whatever balance the society is carrying, e.g. from a savings/FD interest.
  • Inflation (annual) -- a general cost-of-living increase applied on top of every expense category's own trend (and income too, if you switch "Apply Inflation To" in Config below).
  • Contingency Rate (₹/sqft/mo) -- a per-square-foot monthly rate, calculated the same way as Maintenance Rate, used to project the Contingency Fund for forecast months. It defaults to whatever rate was most recently actually charged -- not a plain average -- since contingency genuinely isn't collected every month. This is the only place Contingency appears on this page -- there's no separate Contingency card here anymore, since that would just repeat the figure already shown in full on the Opening & Closing Balance page.
  • Contingency Expenses -- a one-time rupee amount for something unplanned (e.g. an emergency repair), added once, to the very first forecast month.

"Changed" tags and the standard-value tick mark

Every lever remembers its own standard/default value. The moment you move a slider away from that default, an amber "Changed" tag appears right next to its label, and a small caption spells out exactly how far off you are (e.g. "Standard value is ₹4.00 -- you're ₹0.50 below that"). Each slider's track also shows a small grey tick mark exactly where the standard value sits, so you can see the gap visually, not just numerically.

At the top of the page, next to the Config/Reset buttons, a badge reads "N levers changed" -- one glance tells you whether you're looking at the standard projection or a scenario you've been experimenting with, before you even read a single number below. If you haven't changed anything, this badge doesn't show at all.

Configuration (advanced)

Tap "Config" to reveal three methodology settings -- these change how the forecast is calculated, not just its inputs:

  • Reference Window -- how many months of real history to average when calculating each category's starting point (its "baseline"). Default is 12; a newer community with less history will automatically use whatever months are actually available instead. Each category's own lever shows exactly how many months of data its baseline is averaged over (e.g. "avg of 8 months with data") -- a month a category genuinely had no activity in is skipped entirely from its own average, never counted as a ₹0 month.
  • Inflation Combination -- whether inflation and a category's own growth assumption compound together (the standard, slightly higher-impact way) or simply add together (simpler, slightly lower-impact).
  • Apply Inflation To -- whether inflation affects expense categories only (the usual choice) or income categories too.

Income Categories & Expense Categories

These lists are pulled directly from your society's real chart of accounts -- not a fixed list -- so if a category is ever renamed or a new one is added, it shows up here automatically without anyone needing to update this page. Each category shows a Baseline (its average monthly actual, from the Reference Window above, counting only the months it actually has data for) and a slider for how much you expect it to grow or shrink for the rest of the year. Click the "i" next to any category to see exactly how its baseline, your growth %, and inflation combine into its forecasted monthly amount.

To keep the page manageable, only the largest 5 income and 6 expense categories (by size) are shown by default -- use "+ N more categories" to reveal the rest. Categories you haven't expanded still count fully toward the forecast total at their default (0% change) assumption; hiding a slider never hides its money.

Maintenance income itself doesn't appear in this list -- it's already covered by the Maintenance Rate and Maintenance Collection % sliders in Global Assumptions above, so it isn't counted twice.

The result tiles

  • Opening -- the real balance carried forward into the very start of the fiscal year you've selected. Click its "i" for the exact figure; for the technical detail, it's chained from the Closing Balance of the FY right before it, all the way back to the confirmed True Opening Balance anchor -- it is never re-guessed from scratch.
  • Closing -- Opening balance plus this year's full Net Surplus. Click "i" to see Opening + Net Surplus = Closing spelled out with real numbers. This is the cumulative, running total -- it is not the same number as Net Surplus unless the Opening balance happens to be zero.
  • Net Surplus -- this year's income minus this year's expense, on its own (before adding the Opening balance). Click "i" to see Total Income − Total Expense = Net Surplus.
  • Risk -- a quick traffic-light read on whether the projected balance stays healthy through the year (LOW), dips low at some point (MODERATE), or goes negative before year-end (HIGH), based on the lowest projected month-end balance under your current assumptions.

As of Today

A separate section, deliberately independent of whichever FY you have selected above. "Opening" and "Closing" are scoped to the FY you're viewing (which could be a closed year, or a future one that hasn't started) -- this section instead always shows your real, all-time financial position through the most recent month there's actual data for, tagged with an "All-Time · as of [Month 'YY]" badge:

  • Net Operating Surplus -- all real income minus all real expense, since day one. Not filtered by the FY selector, not a forecast -- purely what's actually happened.
  • Bank Balance -- the confirmed True Opening Balance anchor plus that same Net Operating Surplus. This is your real cash position right now, independent of which year you're browsing in the forecast above.

Use this section as your fixed reference point: no matter which FY you switch to above (closed, current, or years into the future), "As of Today" never moves -- it's always telling you where things really stand, today.

Why a month sometimes says "NO DATA" instead of ACTUAL or FORECAST

This page will never invent numbers for a month that has already happened. If a month falls before your society's real record-keeping began (e.g. tracking started partway through a year), that month shows a NO DATA badge with ₹0, not a forecast -- forecasting is only ever applied to months that are still ahead of the most recent real data, never to the past. This keeps closed/historical years honest: a year with only 6 months of real data will correctly show those 6 months as ACTUAL and the other 6 as NO DATA, never quietly fabricated as FORECAST.

Month-by-Month Detail table

Collapsed by default -- tap the row to expand it. Each month is tagged ACTUAL (real recorded figures), FORECAST (projected from your assumptions), or NO DATA (already in the past, but no real record exists -- see above), and the Closing column rolls forward month by month, starting from the true Opening balance above -- so by the final month, Closing reflects everything from the start of the year, not just the forecast months on their own.

Forecast Dashboard (chart)

Two tabs:

  • Trend -- a bar-and-line chart of Income, Expense, and Closing Balance for all 12 months. Actual months are shown in solid colour, forecast months lighter, and NO DATA months in flat grey, so you can see at a glance where real data ends, where genuine gaps sit, and where projection begins. If every category's growth assumption is left at 0%, forecast months will look identical to each other month-to-month -- that's expected, not a bug: with no growth assumption applied, the forecast simply carries the same average forward every month, so the Closing Balance line rises in a straight, constant step rather than a curve. Moving any category slider away from 0% will make that trend curve instead.
  • Expense Mix -- a donut chart showing which categories make up the forecast (not actual) months' spending, so you can see at a glance where the bulk of upcoming expense is concentrated.

Worked example: how the Contingency Rate lever feeds the forecast

Say the Contingency Rate was last actually charged at ₹0.50/sqft in a real month, and your community is 7,01,591 sqft. For any forecast month using that rate: ₹0.50 × 7,01,591 = ₹3,50,796 is added to the reserve that month. Actual months instead use whatever the real recorded rate was for that specific month -- including ₹0 for months it wasn't charged at all. Summed across all 12 months of the selected year, this is a portion of the Closing Balance shown above on this page, not extra money added on top of it -- and it's the same figure you'll see broken down in full on the Opening & Closing Balance page. (You can also just click the Contingency Rate slider's own "i" button to see this exact math with your community's live numbers, instead of doing it by hand.)

How a future year's Opening Balance is calculated (advanced)

If you select a FY that's more than one year away (e.g. it's currently FY 2026-27 and you pick FY 2027-28), that year's Opening Balance isn't just guessed -- it's built by walking month-by-month through every month in between, using the exact same forecast formula shown throughout this page, starting from the last month with real data all the way to the selected year's start. In plain terms: a future year's Opening always equals the (possibly still-forecast) Closing Balance of the year right before it -- it never resets back to a fixed starting number. This also means: if you move a lever, every future year's Opening Balance downstream of today will shift too, since it's genuinely built from your current assumptions, not a cached snapshot.

🆘 Help & Support

Stuck on something? Here's where to get answers without leaving the dashboard.

"Munshi" is not a person -- it's an AI chatbot built into this dashboard, grounded in the contents of this very user guide. Click the floating "Need help?" button in the bottom-right corner of any page, then choose "Chat with Munshi" to ask a question in plain language about whatever you're currently looking at.

For anything that matters -- amounts you're about to act on, figures you plan to quote to other residents -- always cross-check what Munshi tells you against the actual dashboard or the Audited Report, rather than relying on the chat answer alone.

❓ Common Questions
Why does Bank Balance sometimes look different from Opening Balance?

Bank Balance always includes everything up to right now, including whatever period you're currently viewing. Opening Balance specifically shows the balance before your selected period began. So if you're viewing "July only," Bank Balance includes July's activity, but Opening Balance deliberately does not — the difference between the two numbers is simply July's own net savings for that month.

Why is Contingency Cash mentioned on more than one page?

It's the same single reserve fund, just shown from different angles: as a headline "how much has been saved so far" figure on Overview, as part of the Closing Balance breakdown on Opening & Closing, and as a month-by-month growth chart. On Forecasting, it no longer has its own card -- it only appears as the adjustable Contingency Rate lever, so you don't see the same total repeated a third time. Wherever you see it, remember it's part of the Bank Balance / Closing Balance — never extra money on top.

Why do some cards never change when I pick a different date range?

Cards marked with the dashed "All-Time" badge (Bank Balance, Contingency Cash, Corpus, Outstanding Dues) are designed to always show the complete picture from day one to today, regardless of which period you're browsing. Every other card on the dashboard will change to reflect whichever date range is selected at the top of the page.

What does clicking a chart bar or a "clickable" card actually do?

It takes you to the Head Drill-down page, already filtered to exactly what you clicked — so you don't have to manually search for it. It's the fastest way to go from "what's this big number?" to "show me every transaction behind it."

Can I trust the numbers on this dashboard?

Yes — every figure is generated directly from the society's recorded financial transactions, with no manual adjustments made after the fact. The Audited Report on the Overview page is the same officially audited document your management committee is required to share, so you can always cross-check the dashboard against it.

Is Munshi a real person?

No — Munshi is the AI chatbot built into this dashboard (see Help & Support above), not a member of staff. It's currently in Beta, so please double-check anything important against the actual dashboard or the Audited Report rather than relying on it alone.
